Top-20 semantic retrieval review
================================

Scope: top 20 per pattern, with no new date cutoff or keyword exclusions.
60 ranked slots produced 52 unique email IDs and 46 distinct retrieval strings.
40 unique emails precede the reference cutoff of October 16, 2001 Houston time;
12 occur on or after it. This reference flag does not exclude any result.

Initial reviewer: Codex. These are preliminary analyst relevance judgments,
not human-validated annotations or findings of wrongdoing.

Review protocol
---------------
The full saved retrieval text was inspected for each of the 52 unique emails.
Full cleaned context was additionally inspected for the brief forwarding notes
"Comments from Enron Finance" and "Enron stuff", and for "Request of Confirm
Desk". The HTML view exposes full cleaned context for every candidate; that
does not mean every quoted statement has been traced to an original message.

relevant: a concrete lead about a target-pattern component, such as SPE risk
allocation or a connection between financial economics and accounting treatment.
This does not require all three evidence conditions and does not establish intent.
unclear: partial indications, insufficient substantive text, missing attachments,
or a timing/credit question without the specific queried contradiction.
irrelevant: ordinary policy, legal, contract or business language with no concrete
target-pattern behavior in the inspected text. This is not a claim that an email
is irrelevant to every possible investigation.

Review outcomes for the 20 ranked slots in each pattern
-----------------------------------------------------
WHO:      3 relevant, 4 unclear, 13 irrelevant.
HOW MUCH: 4 relevant, 6 unclear, 10 irrelevant.
WHEN:     0 relevant, 5 unclear, 15 irrelevant.

These counts include repeated text under different email IDs and overlapping
emails across patterns. They describe this first screening only. They are not
estimated corpus recall, calibrated score thresholds, or wrongdoing precision.

Findings
--------
WHO top rank 1 (0.7049), email bc1cd95827d5a70d89d21b16457a54b7, reproduces
Enron proxy language describing LJM2 vehicles and arm's-length representations.
It is a useful disclosure/ownership lead. It is not a firsthand admission that
someone knew the disclosure was false.

WHO rank 5 (0.6610), email 4855fa5b2be9477951c04f8bcb30d375, explicitly connects
Enron guarantees, separateness representations and documentation. The writer
asks to make representations accurate before funding; compliance/remediation
is a stated legitimate explanation that must be retained.

HOW MUCH rank 13 (0.5994), email 97e8e939090808cef30067cfb8940da2,
"Rehypothecation of securities", is a strong follow-up lead: Mahonia SPE
collateral arrangements, approximately $600m cash calls, and potential
balance-sheet consequences are discussed together. The writer also asks for
legal/accounting confirmation, so the email alone does not establish deception.

Other follow-up leads include the Lehman forward (HOW MUCH rank 11), Catalytica
spark-spread confirmation (rank 16), and "An UGLY story" (rank 10). The last
quotes a news article and must not be treated as independent insider knowledge.

"Greenfield Summary" ranks 1 for HOW MUCH and WHEN and 12 for WHO. It quantifies
Enron's exposure, expected losses and a proposed sale delay. It does not itself
show a false claim that protection existed at an earlier date. High similarity
across all three patterns is not a substitute for the individual evidence test.

The WHEN top 20 contain ordinary deal cancellations, valuation conventions,
template revisions, collateral disputes and accounting corrections. None was
initially judged a direct retrospective-protection lead. "Weird Deal Change
Yesterday" and the PX reserve message warrant context checks, but explicitly
offer system-error or missed-booking explanations. Those explanations must not
be relabeled as evidence of intentional backdating.

Retrieval limitations visible here
---------------------------------
- WHO ranks 3-4 are repeated risk-policy notices; ranks 7-8 and 10-11 are
  document-preservation notices; ranks 13-15 repeat an organization memo.
  Broad Enron/finance vocabulary and repeated administrative text occupy many
  high ranks. ID deduplication does not remove repeated retrieval content.
- Some top results have almost no new text. WHO rank 2/HOW MUCH rank 2 has only
  a 28-character forwarding note, while the full cleaned message contains
  forwarded ownership/representation questions. Attribution requires care.
- One forwarding note points only to an unavailable newsletter attachment.
  Missing attachments constrain interpretation.
- Original raw evidence archive is not included in this checkout. Full cleaned
  context supports review but does not replace verification against originals.

Recommended next work
---------------------
Reconstruct the Mahonia collateral/accounting thread and the Enron guarantees/
representations correspondence first. Link by original headers/message IDs,
subject, participants, named structures and dates; normalized subject alone is
not proof of a thread. Record risk, knowledge, contradictory action, alternative
explanations and missing material separately.

Before expanding an expensive manual review pool, test more focused variants
for WHEN (actual loss already occurred; request to use an earlier effective date;
retrospective valuation or accounting representation). Preserve the current
three-query baseline and scores for comparison. Decide the date-cutoff policy
explicitly and avoid silently deleting later contextual emails.
