Enron embedding exploration
===========================

Data: 152,885 primary emails, each represented by 256-dimensional
text-embedding-3-small embeddings. No API calls were needed for this analysis.
The supplied email_id keys link plots and representative examples to the source.

Method
------
Vectors were checked for valid shape, finite values, nonzero norm and unique IDs,
then L2-normalized. PCA was fit to all emails. Its first two components explain
9.2% of variance; 50 components explain 55.3%.

MiniBatchKMeans was fit in the original 256-dimensional normalized space, not
on the 2D plot. Among k=6,10,14,18, k=6 had the highest cosine silhouette on a
fixed 2,500-email evaluation sample after training on 40,000 emails. The final
six-group model was refit on all emails; its sampled silhouette was 0.0364.
That low score indicates substantial overlap. Six is an exploratory resolution,
not evidence that this corpus contains exactly six natural categories.

t-SNE used the first 50 PCA dimensions for a seeded random sample of 12,000
emails, perplexity 40 and 1,250 iterations. Seed: 6450. Only 49 known SPE-name
matches occur in this sample, so the all-email PCA overlay and group statistics
provide fuller SPE coverage. Neighborhood trustworthiness was 0.847 on a random
2,000-point subset, using cosine neighborhoods with k=15. It does not measure
global map fidelity. Island sizes and inter-island gaps are not reliable
measures of population share or semantic distance.

Interpretation and next steps
----------------------------
1. Writing style and boilerplate contribute substantially to grouping. Group 2
   has median retrieval length 30 characters, and 66.1% of its emails have fewer
   than 50 characters. Its nearest-centroid examples include "okay" and FYI
   messages. These vectors represent little of the underlying thread's subject.

2. Signature residue is visible. Group 5's distinctive terms include "fax",
   "enron north america" and "corp"; 34.5% contain "phone" or "fax". A typical
   centroid-near email reads "As discussed, please see attached" followed by a
   full corporate signature. This suggests that residual signature language
   influences the map; it does not establish how much of each vector it explains.

3. Known SPE-name matches are dispersed. Group 5 has a 0.765% match rate versus
   0.408% overall, or 1.88x enrichment; it contains 164/623 (26.3%) of matches
   while accounting for 14.0% of the population. Group 0 contains another 153.
   All six groups contain matches, so filtering to one group would lose relevant
   name-based candidates. The broad groups are not wrongdoing classifications.

4. The text-view distinction matters. Of 623 known SPE-name matches in the
   primary baseline, 345 have a qualifying match in body_new_candidate and 278
   match only in quoted/full text. Those quoted-only emails may embed routine
   new messages such as FYI. In Group 2, just 5 of 71 SPE-name matches occur in
   new text. Use original-thread context when reviewing them.

5. Unique emails do not imply unique retrieval text. There are 144,272 distinct
   saved retrieval strings. 11,154 emails (7.3%) share their exact retrieval
   string with another email. "FYI" alone occurs 1,101 times. Repeated short
   strings can create tight neighborhoods without reflecting distinct topics.

Use these maps to inspect examples and guide retrieval rather than to identify
suspicious emails by visual isolation. A useful next comparison would flag
short replies and signatures, inspect SPE nearest neighbors in the original
256D space, and check full threads before changing any preprocessing.
